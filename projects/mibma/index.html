miBMA: Multiple Imputation under Bayesian Model Averaging

When you impute missing data, you have to pick an imputation model, and standard multiple imputation treats that choice as if it were certain. miBMA averages over the plausible imputation models with Bayesian model averaging, so uncertainty about which predictors belong is carried into the imputations. Change the data below and compare it with mice's standard method and with simply dropping incomplete cases.

Paper

An Approach to Addressing Multiple Imputation Model Uncertainty Using Bayesian Model Averaging

David Kaplan & Sinan Yavuz · Multivariate Behavioral Research, 55(4), 553–567 · 2020

Bayesianly proper multiple imputation accounts for uncertainty in the imputation model's parameters but not in the model itself. The paper applies Bayesian model averaging inside the imputation step under the fully conditional specification approach. The miBMA package implements it as a drop-in method for mice: mice(data, method = "miBMA").

Complete case drop missing rows

Analyze only the rows where Y was observed. Simple and fine when data are missing completely at random, but biased when missingness depends on other variables.

mice "norm" standard practice

Bayesian linear regression imputation using every candidate predictor. It accounts for uncertainty in the coefficients but assumes the full model is the right one.

miBMA this research

Scores every subset of predictors by BIC, keeps the plausible ones (Occam's window), and imputes from their posterior-weighted average.

About this demo

Each replication draws equicorrelated standard normal predictors, builds Y from the first k of them plus noise, removes Y for some rows, and runs all three methods. The imputation step mirrors the miBMA package: BIC-weighted averaging over models in Occam's window (odds ratio 20), then the same σ and β draw that mice's "norm" method uses, centered on the averaged coefficients. Results are averaged over 40 replications with 20 imputations each.

To run in your browser, the demo has one incomplete variable, so mice's chained iterations reduce to a single pass, and it searches all predictor subsets directly instead of through regsubsets. Results here illustrate how the methods behave. They aren't the paper's reported results.
